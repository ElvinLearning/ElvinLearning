# Video 2 — The Full Build
### Blank page to finished video, nothing cut out
**Target runtime: 20 minutes · Screen recording + both presenters on camera**

---

## The one job of this video

Prove the whole loop is *fast and boring*. Video 1 sold the magic; this one sells the **repeatability**.
The viewer should finish thinking "I could do that twice a day."

The two moments that matter most:
- **09:00** — re-rolling one bad shot instead of the whole video
- **17:00** — cutting five posts out of one generation

Those two things are what people are actually paying you for. Everything else is setup.

---

## Before you hit record

- [ ] Identity kit from Video 1 ready — **same character, so it feels continuous**
- [ ] All takes pre-generated and organized as `s01.v1.mp4`, `s01.v2.mp4`, etc.
- [ ] **Deliberately keep one genuinely bad take** for the re-roll section at 09:00
- [ ] CapCut open with a blank project, media pre-imported into a folder
- [ ] Music track and caption font already chosen (don't shop for these on camera)
- [ ] A finished version of the video ready to play at 15:00

> Same backup rule as Video 1. Everything pre-generated. Live queues are the enemy of a 20-minute runtime.

## Run of show

### 00:00 – 01:00 · Cold open
**Play the finished 45-second video first. No setup, no talking.**

Then:

> **Quincy:** "That's what we're building in the next twenty minutes. Twelve shots, about fifty
> bucks of generation, and we'll show you every step including the part where one shot comes out
> wrong."

---

### 01:00 – 04:00 · Write the shot graph BEFORE opening any tool
**Screen share: a spreadsheet. Nothing else open.**

> **Kayson:** "The most expensive mistake in AI video is opening a generation tool first."

Build the twelve rows live. Columns: ID · Type · Seconds · Action · Camera · Refs · Takes · Locked.

Narrate the decisions as you go, and make these three explicit:
1. **Shot type drives the model.** Story, b-roll, direct address — different routes.
2. **Nothing over six seconds** except the direct address.
3. **Angle-matched refs** listed per shot — callback to Video 1.

Then do the cost math on screen: `total seconds × candidates × rate`. Land on a real number.

> **Quincy:** "Fifty-six dollars. We know that before we've spent a cent. That's how you quote a
> client without guessing."

---

### 04:00 – 07:00 · Generate the batch
**Screen share: the tool.**

Show routing in practice — don't just say it, do it:
- A story shot → the multi-reference model
- A b-roll shot → the cheap model, no references needed
- The direct-address shot → the audio-driven route

> **Kayson:** "This b-roll shot is a pen on a desk. There's no face in it. Paying premium rates for
> that is just setting money on fire."

Queue everything. Talk over the wait about naming: `s04.v2.mp4`. Nothing overwritten, nothing deleted.

---

### 07:00 – 09:00 · Review and lock
**Screen share: the contact sheet / all takes side by side.**

Watch takes, pick winners, write them down. Do it at pace — this is a rhythm, not a deliberation.

> **Quincy:** "v2. v1. v3. v2. Writing it down is the whole trick — three weeks from now you won't
> remember which one you liked."

---

### 09:00 – 11:00 · THE RE-ROLL (key moment #1)
**Screen share. Slow down here.**

Show the genuinely bad take. Diagnose it out loud — hand artifact, wrong eyeline, whatever it
actually is. Then:

> **Quincy:** "Here's where this system pays for itself. The normal way to fix this is regenerate
> the whole video. Fifty-six dollars, two days. We're going to fix this one shot."

Re-roll **only** that shot. Show the new take. Lock it.

> **Kayson:** "Four dollars. Ninety seconds. The other eleven shots never moved."

Then say the client line explicitly, because it's the thing viewers will resell:

> **Quincy:** "When you're doing this for a client and they say 'I don't love the third shot' —
> that's a same-day fix for four dollars instead of a conversation about budget."

---

### 11:00 – 15:00 · The edit in CapCut
**Screen share: CapCut. This is Kayson's section — let him drive.**

Order matters here. Bring in **clean footage first**, then layer:

1. Drop locked takes on the timeline in order
2. **Voiceover** — lay it under, adjust shot timing to the words, not the reverse
3. **Music** — duck it under dialogue, name the dB
4. **Captions** — burned in, above the platform UI safe zone
5. **On-screen text** — the numbers and key lines
6. **End card** — logo appears here and nowhere earlier

Say the discipline out loud while doing it:

> **Kayson:** "Notice everything I'm adding is a separate layer. No captions baked into the
> generated clips. That's not a preference, that's the thing that makes the next section possible."

Watch the assembled cut once at full speed. Then do the **muted double-speed pass** and explain why:

> **Kayson:** "Face drift and hand problems jump out when you're not listening to the words. Takes
> ninety seconds, catches most of what you'd otherwise ship."

---

### 15:00 – 17:00 · Play the finished piece
**Full screen, sound up, no commentary.**

Then a short beat on the kill criteria — what would have made you not publish this. Be honest if
something is imperfect; say what you'd fix in a real client build.

---

### 17:00 – 19:00 · FIVE POSTS FROM ONE GENERATION (key moment #2)
**Screen share: CapCut, duplicating the timeline.**

This section is worth the price of the course on its own. Move fast and show, don't explain:

| Variant | What you change | Time it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **A** Story cut | The original | — |
| **B** Hook swap | Replace first 3 seconds | ~2 min |
| **C** Short cut | Just the payoff, ~12s | ~3 min |
| **D** Landscape | Recrop to 16:9 | ~2 min |
| **E** Still | Best frame + key line | ~2 min |

Actually build **B** and **D** on screen. Don't just describe them.

> **Quincy:** "Same generation. Five posts. The expensive part is already paid for. If you're
> generating a new video for every post, you're paying five times for something you already own."

> **Kayson:** "Two of these a day is ten posts a day. That's how the math actually works."

---

### 19:00 – 20:00 · Close
**On camera, both of you.**

Recap the loop in one breath: **shot graph → price it → generate three → lock → re-roll failures →
assemble → variants.**

> **Quincy:** "It's seven steps and it should get boring. Boring is the point — boring is what you
> can do every day."

Homework:
- Run the full loop once on a 45-second piece
- Deliberately re-roll one shot so the muscle memory is there
- Cut at least two variants from it

> **Kayson:** "Book Three is the business side — what to charge, how to run a client, the release
> template. Read that one before you take money from anybody."

**End card.**

---

## Deliberate imperfections to leave in

Do not over-polish these recordings. Leave in:
- One take that isn't great, diagnosed honestly
- A moment where you disagree about which take is better
- "Prices change, check current rates"

Polish reads as a sales pitch. A real working session reads as the truth — and it's the reason
someone hires you afterward instead of just buying the course.

## Do not

- Cut out the wait times entirely — acknowledge them, then cut. Pretending it's instant sets people up to feel lied to.
- Promise income
- Say anything that pins the video to a specific tool version you'll have to re-record
