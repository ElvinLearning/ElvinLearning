# Series Bible — the creative engine

The pipeline is the hard part to build. This is the hard part to get *right*. A perfect pipeline
pointed at mediocre concepts produces mediocre videos twice a day, forever.

## The strategic problem

Dr. Boyce's brand is education — financial literacy, Black economic empowerment, The Black Business
School. Education is trustworthy and it is also, in short-form, structurally disadvantaged: a
lesson has no reason to be watched to the end.

Story does. So the format has to be **a story that carries a lesson**, not a lesson decorated with
story. The distinction is everything. If the video still works with the lesson removed, the lesson
is decoration. If removing the lesson breaks the ending, you have it right.

Serialized content is the 2026 play: 57% of consumers prefer brands that post original content
series, and consistency of character, location and visual language across ~10 episodes is what
converts a viewer into an audience. Do not post 60 unrelated videos a month. Post **three
recognizable shows.**
([Medium](https://medium.com/@yashasvi_nurdd/episodic-short-form-series-why-creators-are-abandoning-the-one-off-reel-in-2026-bdc6aa03ed9f))

## The three shows

Two videos a day = one from the flagship, one rotating from the other two.

### 1. "The Lesson" — flagship, Boyce as side character *(60% of output)*

A short dramatic scene. Someone is about to make a money mistake. Boyce is **not** the protagonist —
he's the barber, the guy at the next table, the contractor, the woman's uncle, the man in line
behind them. He says **one thing.** The scene turns. Cut to him direct-to-camera for eight seconds
of the actual lesson.

Why side character: it's more rewatchable, it's less ego-forward (which paradoxically builds more
authority), it lets the viewer be the protagonist, and it gives you an infinite premise generator —
any money mistake anyone has ever made is an episode.

**Beat sheet (52s):**
| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0–3s | Mid-conflict. Someone says the wrong thing about money, out loud, with confidence. |
| Stakes | 3–12s | Why this matters. Show the number. |
| Escalation | 12–25s | They double down. The other person nods along. It's going to happen. |
| The turn | 25–33s | Boyce, from the edge of frame, says one sentence. |
| Reframe | 33–45s | The room changes. Show the real math. |
| Direct address | 45–52s | Boyce to camera. The lesson, named. |

**Premise bank:** the tax refund celebration · co-signing for family · the depreciating status
purchase · "my job is my security" · the church-adjacent investment pitch · the 700-credit-score
flex with no assets · the inheritance that evaporated in 18 months · leasing to look successful ·
"I'll start investing when I make more" · the business with no separate bank account.

### 2. "Boyce vs." — comedy, Boyce as main character *(25%)*

Boyce dropped into an absurd, high-stakes, visually rich scenario where financial competence is the
superpower. A heist crew that needs a CFO. A dragon guarding a hoard with terrible asset
allocation. A time-traveler who wants to invest in 1985. A billionaire's will read to seven
relatives. Genre and spectacle carry the reach; the punchline is always a real principle.

This is where the money goes — Veo 3.1 hero shots, real production value. One of these landing is
worth thirty of everything else.

### 3. "The Millionaire Next Door" — docu-drama *(15%)*

90-second mini-biopics of Black wealth builders, historical and contemporary. Boyce narrates and
appears *in period* as an observer. Highest brand equity, most shareable to older audiences, best
long-tail on YouTube. Also the most defensible content on the channel — nobody can accuse a
history piece of being AI slop when it teaches something real.

## Hook rules (non-negotiable)

The first three seconds decide everything, and strong three-second holds compound: the algorithm
starts trusting the account and pushes subsequent uploads harder.
([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas))

1. **Open mid-scene.** Never establish. Never "In this video." The first frame is already conflict.
2. **First line is spoken by the person who is wrong.** Viewers stay to see someone corrected.
3. **A number in the first five seconds.** "$40,000." Specificity beats adjectives.
4. **Boyce is not in the first shot** in The Lesson. His arrival is the payoff.
5. **No logo, no intro, no name card** until the last two seconds.

## Retention rules

- Cut every 2–4 seconds. No shot over 6 seconds except the direct address.
- Never repeat a camera setup inside an episode.
- Show the money as on-screen text at the moment it's said, always.
- The lesson lands in the last 8 seconds and is never pre-announced.
- End on a statement, not a question. Questions invite scroll; statements invite comment.

## The variant engine (how one generation becomes five posts)

Generation is expensive; assembly is free. Every episode ships as:

| Variant | What changes | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A — Story cut | The full 52s | TikTok, Reels, Shorts |
| B — Hook swap | Different first 3s, same body | Re-post 6 days later |
| C — Lesson cut | Direct-address only, 12s, captioned | Reels, LinkedIn |
| D — Landscape | 16:9 recrop, longer breathing room | YouTube, X |
| E — Carousel still | Key frame + the number + the lesson | Instagram, LinkedIn |

Two generated episodes a day, produced this way, is **ten posts a day** across platforms without a
single extra inference call. That is the actual answer to "two videos a day" — and it is why the
captions, music and titles are never baked into generation.

## Quality bar — kill an episode if

- His face differs between any two shots (re-roll, don't ship).
- Any hand does something impossible in a shot the viewer will look at.
- Lip-sync drifts on the direct address (that's the shot people screenshot).
- The lesson could be removed and the video still works.
- It reads as a lecture with pictures.

Two great videos a day builds the brand. Two mediocre ones a day trains the algorithm that the
account is low quality, and that is very hard to undo. **When in doubt, ship one.**
